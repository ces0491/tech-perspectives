Olmo 3's assistant version uses "isn't just" in about a quarter of the 4,000 blog posts I had it write. That is roughly sixty times the rate American writers used it on the web in 2012.

In May I argued that the "AI sound" is averaged humanness: a model writes like the centre of millions of human writers, so its tells are the most common patterns in human writing. I never tested it. So I did, across four studies, each registered before its own data existed.

An average can't sit outside the range of the things it averages. This does. The model runs far above every country I measured on some constructions, below all of them on others, and three of the fifteen never appeared at all in 10 million words of its writing. Whatever produces the sound, averaging isn't it.

The contrast rises at every stage of the model's training after the first, and none of the text it trained on uses it at anything close to the rate it ends up at.
